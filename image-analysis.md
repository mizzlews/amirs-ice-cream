# Reference image analysis — ice cream cone

Reference: `assets/ice-cream-cone-with-spoon.jpg` (Thegreatmutata, CC0, Wikimedia Commons).
Single three-quarter view, low evening sun from the upper left, street pavement behind.

## Macro
An inverted truncated cone held point-down, carrying two stacked near-spherical scoop masses.
Overall silhouette: a narrow wedge that widens into an irregular double dome.

## Meso
- **Cone body** — right circular cone, apex down, embossed with a diamond waffle lattice.
  Roughly 18 cells around, 14 down; ridge crowns rounded, relief about 1 mm on a 70 mm top diameter.
- **Rim frill** — a thin flared collar at the cone's mouth, scalloped and wavy, pale on the inner
  face and toasted on the outer. It is what separates a waffle cone from a wafer cone.
- **Lower scoop** — a squat dome, wider than tall, with one horizontal shear line left by the scoop.
  Surface smooth-matte, few tears.
- **Upper scoop** — an irregular ball with a cauliflower surface: crests, folds and torn edges,
  one lip overhanging the cone on the left.
- **Paper sleeve, hand, plastic spoon** — present in the reference, out of scope for the page model.

## Micro
Biscuit chunks 3–8 mm; a darker ribbon swirl 2–4 mm wide folded through the upper scoop;
air pockets and pits; a melt gloss on the sunlit upper left; crumbs on the crests.

## Materials, in PBR terms
- Waffle cone: dielectric, base colour #e8c389 in the valleys to #c8833f on the ridge crowns,
  roughness about 0.75, no metalness.
- Frill: the same body, lighter (#ecd9b4), thin enough to glow at the edge.
- Ice cream: dielectric with strong subsurface response; approximated with roughness 0.55–0.7,
  a warm sheen and a very low clearcoat for the melt gloss.
- Inclusions: matte tan, no gloss.

## Identity-defining features
1. The diamond waffle lattice on the cone.
2. The scalloped, flared rim frill.
3. The torn cauliflower surface of the upper scoop — not a smooth sphere.
4. Two scoops, the lower one squatter and sheared.
5. The warm toast gradient, darker on the ridge crowns.

## What a single view cannot show
The back of the cone, the tip (hidden by the hand), the undersides of both scoops, and the
true depth of the ribbon. These are inferred, stated here rather than claimed as measured.
